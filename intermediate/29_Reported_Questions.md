# 29. Reported Questions

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reported questions** | Questions converted into indirect speech to report what someone previously asked | Непрямі (передані) запитання |
| **Direct speech** | The exact words spoken by a person enclosed in quotation marks | Пряма мова |
| **Indirect speech** | Reporting what someone said or asked without quoting their exact words | Непряма мова |
| **Backshifting** | Shifting verb tenses one step back into the past when reporting speech | Зсув часів у минуле |
| **Direct word order** | Standard affirmative sentence structure (Subject + Verb) used in reported questions | Прямий порядок слів |
| **Reporting verb** | A verb used to introduce reported speech or questions (e.g., ask, wonder, inquire) | Дієслово передачі мовлення (ask, wonder) |
| **WH-question** | A question starting with a question word such as who, what, where, when, why, or how | Спеціальне запитання (з Wh-словом) |
| **Yes/No question** | A question that can be answered with a simple affirmative or negative response | Загальне запитання (так/ні) |
| **Time expression change** | Adjusting time markers (e.g., yesterday \\(\rightarrow\\) the day before) in backshifting | Зміна часових вказівників |
| **If / Whether** | Conjunctions used to connect Yes/No questions in reported speech | Сполучник «чи» (if / whether) |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "When turning direct questions into **reported questions**, drop auxiliary *do/does/did* and use **direct word order** with **backshifting**!"

> "For Yes/No questions, connect the reporting clause with **if** or **whether**, and shift the tense one step into the past!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (WH- Questions): `Somebody asked + WH-word / phrase + Subject + Verb (Past Tense)`  
Formula (Yes/No Questions): `Somebody asked + if / whether + Subject + Verb (Past Tense)`  
Formula (Backshifting Chart — One Step Back in Tenses):  
* `Present Simple` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Past Simple` (*"Do you like coffee?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *He asked if I liked coffee*)  
* `Present Continuous` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Past Continuous` (*"Why are you laughing?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *She asked why I was laughing*)  
* `Present Perfect` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Past Perfect` (*"Have you finished?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *They asked if I had finished*)  
* `Past Simple` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Past Perfect` (*"Where did you learn English?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *She asked where I had learned English*)  
* `Will` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Would` | `Can` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Could` | `May` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Might` | `Must` \\(\rightarrow\\) `Had to`  

* **Правила утворення непрямих запитань (Reported Questions Rules):**  
  1. **Прямий порядок слів (Direct Word Order):** У непрямих запитаннях підмет ставиться перед присудком (як у стверджувальному реченні). Допоміжні дієслова *do, does, did* вилучаються (*"Where do you live?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *He asked where I lived*).  
  2. **Зсув часів у минуле (Backshifting):** Якщо головне дієслово стоїть у минулому часі (*asked, wanted to know*), час у підрядній частині змінюється на один крок назад у минуле.  
  3. **Запитання Yes/No:** У загальних запитаннях додаються сполучники **if** або **whether** (*"Can you swim?"* \\(\rightarrow\\) *He asked if I could swim*).  
  4. **Зміна вказівників часу та займенників:** Займенники та вказівники часу змінюються відповідно до контексту (*yesterday* \\(\rightarrow\\) *the day before*, *last week* \\(\rightarrow\\) *the previous week*).

*Example: Direct: "Where do you work?" \\(\rightarrow\\) Reported: She asked me where I worked.*  
*Example: Direct: "Are you available?" \\(\rightarrow\\) Reported: He asked if I was available.*  
*Example: Direct: "What time does the train leave?" \\(\rightarrow\\) Reported: She asked what time the train left.*  
*Example: Direct: "Can you help me?" \\(\rightarrow\\) Reported: He asked whether I could help him.*  

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Put the Words in the Correct Order

1. asked / where / lived / she / I  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **where I lived**.
2. me / if / asked / available / was / I / he  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked **if I was available**.
3. asked / they / me / started / the meeting / when /  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) They asked me **when the meeting started** (або: Can you tell me **when the meeting starts**?).
4. asked / she / whether / they / arrived / had  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **whether they had arrived**.
5. asked / why / upset / was / he / I  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked **why I was upset**.

### Exercise 2: Transform the Sentences into Reported Speech

1. "Where do you work?"  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked me **where I worked**.
2. "Are you tired?"  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked me **if I was tired**.
3. "What time does the train leave?"  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **what time the train left**.
4. "Can you help me?"  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked **if I could help him**.
5. "Why are you laughing?"  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **why I was laughing**.

### Exercise 3: Find and Correct the Mistakes

**Part 1:**
1. He asked me what was my favourite subject.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked me **what my favourite subject was**.
2. They asked if was I ready for the presentation.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) They asked **if I was ready for the presentation**.
3. She asked where had I learned English.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **where I had learned English**.
4. My manager asked me whether could I finish the report by Friday.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) My manager asked me **whether I could finish the report by Friday**.
5. He asked why was I laughing.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked **why I was laughing**.

**Part 2:**
1. She asked me if I can help her with the project.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked me **if I could help her with the project**.
2. They asked what time did the meeting start.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) They asked **what time the meeting started** (або **had started**).
3. My friend asked me whether I had seen his message yesterday.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) My friend asked me **whether I had seen his message the day before**.
4. He asked me where was the nearest bus stop.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) He asked me **where the nearest bus stop was**.
5. She asked if I enjoyed the conference last week.  
   \\(\rightarrow\\) She asked **if I had enjoyed the conference the previous week**.

### Translation Block

* Вона запитала мене, де я працюю. — *She asked me **where I worked**.*
* Він запитав, чи я втомився після роботи. — *He asked me **if I was tired** after work.*
* Мій менеджер запитав, чи зможу я закінчити звіт до п'ятниці. — *My manager asked me **whether I could finish the report by Friday**.*
* Вона запитала, де я навчився так вільно розмовляти англійською. — *She asked **where I had learned to speak English so fluently**.*
* Вони запитали, о котрій годині розпочалася зустріч. — *They asked **what time the meeting started**.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: How often do you need to report questions or summarize client inquiries at work?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I report client questions daily in our team status meetings, especially when summarizing project requirements.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the project stage; I report client inquiries when there are technical changes to confirm.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I don't write summary reports often, as most of our client communication is direct and documented automatically.

**Q: Do you find backshifting verb tenses natural or do you still hesitate with past tenses?**
* **Positive:** Actually, applying backshifting feels very natural now once you remember to step tenses back into the past.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I sometimes hesitate with Past Perfect shifts, but keeping direct word order is my main focus.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I still make minor tense errors when speaking fast under time pressure.

**Q: Which conjunction do you prefer using for Yes/No reported questions — "if" or "whether"?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I use "whether" in formal business emails because it sounds slightly more professional, though "if" is great for conversation.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I use "if" most of the time because it's shorter and quicker to say.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I sometimes mix them up or forget to switch to direct word order after "whether".

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Reporting Client Inquiries to a Team Lead**
* **Context:** A project manager reporting client questions from a morning briefing to an engineering lead.
* **Role A:** "Could you summarize what the client asked during this morning's kickoff call?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes! **She asked where** our team was hosted and **whether we could finish the report by Friday**."
* **Role A:** "Did they inquire about the integration schedule as well?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, yes. **They asked when the deployment started** and **if I had tested the new database backup**."

**Scenario 2: Clarifying Job Interview & Candidate Details**
* **Context:** An HR specialist debriefing a department manager after a candidate interview.
* **Role A:** "How did the interview go with the senior developer candidate?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, very well! **I asked him where he had learned English** and **why he was interested** in joining our company."
* **Role A:** "Did he have any questions regarding our team structure?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes. **He asked if he could work remotely** and **what time the daily standup meeting started**."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **She asked where I...** | Standard, natural formula for reporting WH-questions in business or casual settings. |
| **He asked if / whether I could...** | Polite, professional formula for reporting Yes/No requests or possibilities. |
| **They wanted to know if...** | Used when relaying client or manager inquiries gently during team updates. |
| **To be honest, they asked...** | Softener used when sharing unexpected or challenging questions asked by stakeholders. |
| **Actually, she inquired whether...** | A polished, professional softener for reporting formal business questions. |
