# 05. Present Perfect Continuous

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Duration** | The length of time that an activity or situation continues | Тривалість |
| **Ongoing activity** | An action that is currently in progress or has been continuing over a period | Поточна / тривала діяльність |
| **Present result** | A visible effect or physical evidence in the present caused by a recent action | Теперішній результат / видимий наслідок |
| **Stative verbs** | Verbs that express a state, feeling, or mental condition rather than a physical action | Дієслова стану (статичні дієслова) |
| **For** | A preposition used to indicate the length or total extent of a time period | Протягом (певного періоду) |
| **Since** | A preposition used to reference a specific starting point in time in the past | З (певного моменту у минулому) |
| **Out of breath** | Breathing heavily or with difficulty as a result of recent physical exertion | Захеканий, зі збитим диханням |
| **Covered in paint** | Having paint smeared or spread all over one's body or clothing | Весь у фарбі / запруджений фарбою |
| **Unwind** | To relax and reduce tension after a long period of work or activity | Відпочити, розслабитися |
| **Consistently** | Continuing to happen or develop in the same way over a period of time | Послідовно, регулярно |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Use **Present Perfect Continuous** to focus on **how long** you've been working, but switch to **Present Perfect Simple** to show **how much** you've done!"

> "Count the total period of time with **for**, but point to the exact starting moment with **since**!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula: `Subject + have / has + been + Verb+ing`
Formula (Negative): `Subject + haven't / hasn't + been + Verb+ing`
Formula (Question): `(How long) + have / has + Subject + been + Verb+ing?`

* **Дія, що розпочалася в минулому і триває досі:** Час Present Perfect Continuous використовується для опису тривалих дій, які почалися раніше і все ще продовжуються у момент мовлення. Для вказівки часових рамок часто використовуються часові маркери *for* (протягом якого часу) та *since* (з якого саме моменту).
* **Дія, яка щойно завершилася і має видимий результат:** Вживається для опису діяльності у недавньому минулому, процес якої призвів до очевидних фізичних або емоційних наслідків у теперішньому (наприклад, втома, задишка, брудні руки).
* **Різниця між Present Perfect та Present Perfect Continuous:**
  * **Present Perfect Simple** підкреслює **завершеність** та **результат** або кількість зробленого (*how many / how much*).
  * **Present Perfect Continuous** підкреслює **тривалість**, процес або видимі сліди діяльності (*how long*).
* **Статичні дієслова (Stative Verbs):** Дієслова, що описують стан, емоції, володіння чи розумові процеси (*know, love, believe, want, understand, have*), зазвичай **не використовуються** в часах Continuous. Для них замість Present Perfect Continuous уживається Present Perfect Simple (*I have known her for three years*, а не *I have been knowing*).

*Example: I have been studying English for two years.*
*Example: He has been working at the company since 2014.*
*Example: I have been working on the project all night, so I am very tired now.*
*Example: She has read ten books this summer, but she has been reading that book all day.*

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Complete the Sentences (Present Perfect Continuous)

1. She **has been studying** for her exams all week.
2. They **have been playing** soccer since this morning.
3. I **have been waiting** for you for over an hour.
4. He **has been painting** the house, so he is covered in paint.
5. We **haven't been working** on this project since last month.
6. You **have been learning** English for several years now.
7. The children **haven't been watching** TV all afternoon.
8. It **has been raining** all day, so the streets are wet.
9. She **hasn't been cooking** dinner for the past two hours.
10. He **has been driving** for six hours and needs a break.

### Exercise 2: Present Perfect Simple vs Present Perfect Continuous

1. She **has been studying** French for three years.
2. I **have finished** my homework, so now I can relax.
3. They **have been working** on that project all day, and they are still not done.
4. He **has read** five books this month.
5. We **have been living** in this neighborhood for ten years.
6. You look tired. **Have** you **been running**?
7. She **has made** dinner, and it smells delicious.
8. He **has been writing** emails since he got to the office this morning.
9. I **have traveled** to France several times.
10. The children **have been playing** outside for hours.

### Exercise 3: Contextual Action vs Result Prompts

1. They **have been playing** football for 30 minutes. / A football player **has scored** a goal.
2. She **has been cooking** for 2 hours. / She **has made** a cake.
3. He **has written** 10 emails. / She **has been working** all morning.
4. He **has been fishing** all day. / He **has caught** a fish.
5. She **has been taking** exams for 2 days. / They **have passed** exams and they feel happy.
6. She **has been cleaning** her flat for 6 hours.

### Exercise 4: Find and Correct the Mistakes

1. She have been working here since five years. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **has** been working here **for** five years.
2. I have learning English for two months. \\(\rightarrow\\) I have **been** learning English for two months.
3. They been playing football all afternoon. \\(\rightarrow\\) They **have** been playing football all afternoon.
4. You have been waiting for a long time? \\(\rightarrow\\) **Have you** been waiting for a long time?
5. We have been study for the exam all day. \\(\rightarrow\\) We have been **studying** for the exam all day.
6. It has been rained since morning. \\(\rightarrow\\) It has been **raining** since morning.

### Translation Block

* Вона готується до іспитів увесь тиждень. — *She **has been studying** for her exams all week.*
* Я чекаю на тебе вже понад годину. — *I **have been waiting** for you for over an hour.*
* Вони працюють над цим проєктом увесь день і все ще не закінчили. — *They **have been working** on that project all day, and they are still not done.*
* Він написав 10 електронних листів з того часу, як прийшов в офіс. — *He **has written** 10 emails since he got to the office this morning.*
* Вона вивчає французьку мову протягом трьох років. — *She **has been studying** French for three years.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: How long have you been learning English?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I’d say I have been learning English for about two years now, and I’ve noticed a huge improvement in my speaking skills recently.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have been studying English off and on for several years, though I recently decided to focus on it more consistently.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I have only been learning English for a few months, so I still find complex grammar topics quite challenging.

**Q: How long have you been living in your current city or town?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have been living in my current city for over ten years, and I absolutely love the cultural life and friendly community here.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have been residing here for roughly three years due to work commitments, which feels like a reasonable amount of time.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I have only been living here for a few weeks, so I haven't really had the chance to explore the area properly yet.

**Q: What book have you been reading these days?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have been reading a fascinating book on leadership strategies for the past two weeks, and it offers great professional insights.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have been reading a modern detective novel recently whenever I manage to find a quiet moment in the evening.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't been reading any books lately because my daily work schedule has been extremely demanding.

**Q: How long have you been working in your current profession or company?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have been working in my current industry for nearly five years, and I find the ongoing professional growth very fulfilling.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have been with my company for about eighteen months, which has been a steady learning experience.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I have only been working at this position for two months, so I am still adjusting to the workload and team dynamics.

**Q: What project or task have you been spending most of your time on recently?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have been focusing on developing a new marketing campaign for our team, which has been an exciting process.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have been dedicating most of my working hours to reviewing routine financial reports and updating client databases.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I have been spending far too much time answering repetitive administrative emails rather than working on strategic tasks.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Weekly Project Progress Meeting**
* **Context:** A project manager checks in with a lead software engineer about current sprint tasks.
* **Role A:** "Could you give me a brief update on the database migration task? How long have you been working on it?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, our engineering team **has been optimizing** the core database structure since Monday, and we have already completed most of the data checks."
* **Role A:** "That sounds promising. Have you encountered any technical difficulties during the process?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, we **have been testing** different server configurations all morning, but we expect to finish everything by 5 p.m."

**Scenario 2: Employee Performance & Workload Check-In**
* **Context:** A department head discussing workload and energy levels with a team member.
* **Role A:** "You look a bit exhausted today. Would you mind telling me what you've been working on?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, I **have been preparing** the annual financial presentation for three consecutive days, so I feel slightly drained."
* **Role A:** "I completely understand. You **have written** several thorough reports this week, so perhaps you could take a short break this afternoon?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, that would be very helpful. I appreciate your consideration."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to share positive achievements, gently correct assumptions, or present helpful extra context politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when admitting workload pressure, sharing neutral progress updates, or stating personal facts directly. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to communicate minor delays, lack of time, or temporary difficulties in a soft, courteous manner. |
| **Could you tell me...** | An indirect, polite formula used to request updates or ask team members about ongoing tasks. |
| **I'd say that...** | Used to express a personal estimate or opinion about duration and timelines modestly. |
| **I've noticed that...** | A non-confrontational way to bring up observations regarding workload, fatigue, or performance. |
