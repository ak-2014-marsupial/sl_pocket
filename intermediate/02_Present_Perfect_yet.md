# 02. Present Perfect (yet, already, just)

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Perfect** | A verb tense used to express actions that happened at an unspecified time in the past or have a direct connection to the present result | Теперішній доконаний час |
| **Just** | A short time ago; very recently | Щойно, тільки-но |
| **Already** | Before now or earlier than expected | Вже |
| **Yet** | Up to the present time; used in negative sentences and questions to indicate expectation | Ще не (в запереченнях), вже (у питаннях) |
| **Definite time** | A specific, known point or period in the past (used with Past Simple) | Визначений / точний час |
| **Indefinite time** | An unspecified or unknown time in the past (used with Present Perfect) | Невизначений / неокреслений час |
| **Experience** | Knowledge, skill, or events gained through living or doing things over time | Досвід |
| **Result** | An outcome, consequence, or visible effect produced by a recent action | Результат, наслідок |
| **Miss the bus** | To arrive too late to catch a bus | Запізнитися на автобус |
| **Log off** | To disconnect from a computer system, application, or network account | Вийти з системи / акаунту |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Use **just** for actions finished a moment ago, **already** for tasks completed earlier than expected, and **yet** for things you are still waiting to do!"

> "Past Simple asks **when** an action happened, but Present Perfect looks at the **result** in your hands right now!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula: `Subject + have / has + Verb+ed / Verb3`
Formula (Negative): `Subject + haven't / hasn't + Verb+ed / Verb3`
Formula (Question): `Have / Has + Subject + Verb+ed / Verb3 ?`

* **Основне значення Present Perfect:** Вживається для опису дій, які відбулися в минулому, але точний час не вказується (indefinite time), або коли фокус робиться на результаті цієї дії у теперішньому (present result).
* **Слова-маркери часу (Time Expressions):**
  * **Just** (щойно, тільки-но) — стоїть між допоміжним дієсловом *have/has* та основним дієсловом у стверджувальних реченнях.
  * **Already** (вже) — ставиться між *have/has* та основним дієсловом у ствердженнях, коли дія відбулася раніше, ніж очікувалося.
  * **Yet** (ще не — у запереченнях, вже — у питаннях) — завжди ставиться наприкінці речення.
* **Різниця між Present Perfect та Past Simple:**
  * **Past Simple:** вказує на точний час у минулому (*yesterday, last week, in 2010*), послідовність завершених подій або минулі звички.
  * **Present Perfect:** описує життєвий досвід (*ever, never*), неокреслений час або поточний результат.

*Example: I have just finished my homework, so I can go for a walk now.*
*Example: She has already left the office for lunch.*
*Example: Have you sent the email yet? I haven't completed my report yet.*
*Example: I saw that movie last week (Past Simple), but I have already seen that movie twice (Present Perfect).*

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Open the Brackets in Present Perfect

1. I **haven't eaten** sushi before, so I'm excited to try it tonight.
2. **Have** you **met** a famous person?
3. She **has done** her homework, so now she can watch TV.
4. They **haven't visited** Paris.
5. We **have seen** that movie twice. Let's watch something new.
6. He **has finished** a project before the deadline.
7. I **have not decided** what to wear.
8. **Have** you **tried** the new restaurant in town?

### Exercise 2: Finish the Sentences with "already / just / yet"

1. I have **already** been to New York City once. I would like to visit it again in the future!
2. My cousin has **just** called me and asked for my help.
3. Has your colleague sent you the meeting summary **yet**?
4. We haven't found another apartment **yet**.
5. Do you want to watch a new movie with Ryan Gosling together? - No, thank you, I have **already** watched it.

### Exercise 3: Correct the Mistakes

1. I have just eat lunch, but I'm still so hungry. \\(\rightarrow\\) I have just **eaten** lunch, but I'm still so hungry.
2. Have you finished your homework already? \\(\rightarrow\\) Have you finished your homework **yet**?
3. They didn't arrive yet; they're still on the way. \\(\rightarrow\\) They **haven't arrived** yet; they're still on the way.
4. The guests has just left, and the house is finally quiet. \\(\rightarrow\\) The guests **have** just left, and the house is finally quiet.
5. Have you spoke to the teacher about your grades yet? \\(\rightarrow\\) Have you **spoken** to the teacher about your grades yet?

### Exercise 4: Past Simple vs Present Perfect

1. I **saw** that movie last week.
2. She **hasn't finished** her homework yet.
3. We **met** in 2010 at a conference.
4. They **have just bought** a new car.
5. I **lived** in London last summer.
6. **Have** you ever **tried** sushi?
7. He **didn't call** me yesterday.
8. We **have been** to that museum several times.
9. She **graduated** from university two years ago.
10. I **have already read** that book. It's amazing!

### Exercise 5: Image-Based Cause & Effect Prompts (Why Questions)

1. Why is he angry? — He **has just missed** the bus.
2. Why is she hungry? — She **hasn't eaten** anything **yet**.
3. Why is he tired? — He **has worked** a lot.
4. Why is he smiling? — He **has met** an old friend.
5. Why is she so relaxed? — She **has already finished** all the work.
6. Why is she excited? — She **has just bought** a new car.
7. Why are they nervous? — They **have never flown** on a plane.

### Translation Block

* Я тільки-но закінчив домашнє завдання. — *I **have just finished** my homework.*
* Вона щойно пішла з офісу. — *She **has just left** the office.*
* Ви вже надіслали підсумок зустрічі? — *Have you sent the meeting summary **yet**?*
* Ми познайомилися у 2010 році на конференції. — *We **met** in 2010 at a conference.*
* Вона вже закінчила всю роботу, тому вона така розслаблена. — *She **has already finished** all the work, so she is so relaxed.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: Have you started working on the new project yet?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have already started drafting the project proposal, and I've just shared the preliminary timeline with the team.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I haven't had much time to focus on it yet, but I plan to review the requirements this afternoon.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't started working on it yet because I have been dealing with several urgent client requests.

**Q: Have you ever traveled to another country?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have visited several countries in Europe, and I have recently returned from a wonderful trip to Italy.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have only been abroad once or twice, but I am currently planning a vacation for next summer.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't had the chance to travel abroad yet, though I would certainly love to visit Japan one day.

**Q: Have you ever eaten a strange or unusual food?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I tried oysters during my trip to France last year, and I must admit I found them surprisingly delicious.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I haven't really eaten anything particularly unusual yet, as I usually prefer sticking to traditional dishes.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't tried any exotic food yet because I tend to be rather cautious when ordering new meals.

**Q: Have you ever won a competition or a prize?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I won first place in a local hackathon last year, which was an extraordinarily exciting experience for our team.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I haven't won any major competitions recently, though I received a small recognition award at work last month.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't won any prizes yet, to be honest, as I rarely enter competitive events.

**Q: Have you ever forgotten someone's birthday?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I rarely forget birthdays because I keep all my important dates organized in a digital calendar.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I have forgotten a colleague's birthday once or twice, but I managed to apologize politely and send a message later.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I have forgotten a friend's birthday recently, which was quite embarrassing, to be honest.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Status Update on Deliverables**
* **Context:** A project manager asking an associate about important client emails and deliverables.
* **Role A:** "Could you please update me on the revised proposal? Have you sent it to the client yet?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, I have **just** emailed the document to them, and I have **already** notified the executive team."
* **Role A:** "That's great news. Have you received any feedback regarding the budget schedule yet?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, they haven't replied **yet**, but I expect a response by the end of the day."

**Scenario 2: Preparing for a Meeting**
* **Context:** Two colleagues discussing project progress before a weekly sync.
* **Role A:** "Have you started preparing the presentation slides for tomorrow's meeting yet?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, I have **already** finished the first draft, but I haven't checked the financial figures **yet**."
* **Role A:** "Would you like me to review those numbers with you now? I have **just** received the updated report from accounting."
* **Role B:** "That would be very helpful, thank you! I'd really appreciate your feedback."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to introduce positive updates, share completed tasks politely, or gently correct assumptions. |
| **To be honest** | Used when admitting that a task isn't completed yet or sharing candid personal opinions gracefully. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to soften negative answers, express polite regret, or report delays gently. |
| **Have you happened to...** | A polite, non-confrontational formula for inquiring about status updates or completed tasks. |
| **I'm happy to report that...** | Used in professional contexts to deliver good news about reaching a milestone or completing a goal. |
| **Could you please update me on...** | An indirect, respectful way to request information regarding project deadlines and progress. |
