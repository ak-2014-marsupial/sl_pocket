# 41. Question Tags

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Question tag** | A short question added at the end of a statement to confirm information or seek agreement | Разділове запитання («хвостик») |
| **Auxiliary verb** | A verb used with a main verb to express tense, mood, or voice (e.g., *do, be, have*) | Допоміжне дієслово |
| **Statement** | A definite or clear expression of something in speech or writing | Твердження, висловлювання |
| **Confirmation** | The action of establishing or proving the truth or correctness of something | Підтвердження, перевірка інформації |
| **Imperative** | A grammatical mood expressing a command, request, or instruction | Наказовий спосіб |
| **Negative tag** | A question tag containing a negative verb form (*isn't, don't, won't*) | Заперечний «хвостик» |
| **Positive tag** | A question tag containing an affirmative verb form (*is, do, will*) | стверджувальний «хвостик» |
| **Intonation** | The rise and fall of the voice in speaking, which changes the meaning of question tags | Інтонація, тон голосу |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Opposites attract: a **positive statement** takes a **negative tag**, and a **negative statement** takes a **positive tag**!"

> "When using **I am**, forget *am I not* and say **aren't I?**"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Positive Statement): `Subject + Auxiliary Verb + Main Verb, Auxiliary Verb + not + Subject Pronoun ?`
Formula (Negative Statement): `Subject + Auxiliary Verb + not + Main Verb, Auxiliary Verb + Subject Pronoun ?`

* **Визначення та призначення:** Question tags (розділові запитання / «хвостики») — це короткі запитання, які додаються наприкінці стверджувальних або заперечних речень. Вони використовуються для підтвердження інформації або залучення співрозмовника до діалогу.
* **Базове правило («Плюс на мінус, мінус на плюс»):**
  * Стверджувальне речення (+) \\(\rightarrow\\) Заперечний хвостик (-)
  * Заперечне речення (-) \\(\rightarrow\\) Стверджувальний хвостик (+)
* **Узгодження часів та допоміжних дієслів:**
  * **Present Simple:** *She likes coffee, **doesn’t she**? / They don’t eat meat, **do they**?*
  * **Past Simple:** *They visited the museum, **didn’t they**? / She didn’t enjoy the film, **did she**?*
  * **Present Continuous:** *He is studying hard, **isn’t he**? / You aren’t coming, **are you**?*
  * **Past Continuous:** *We were watching TV, **weren’t we**? / He wasn’t sleeping, **was he**?*
  * **Present Perfect:** *You’ve been to Paris, **haven’t you**? / She hasn’t finished, **has she**?*
  * **Past Perfect:** *He had completed the report, **hadn’t he**? / They hadn’t seen it, **had they**?*
  * **Future Simple:** *They will arrive soon, **won’t they**? / You won’t forget, **will you**?*
* **Винятки та особливі випадки (Exceptions):**
  * **I am** \\(\rightarrow\\) **aren’t I?** (*I am younger than you, **aren’t I**?*)
  * **Imperative (Наказ/прохання)** \\(\rightarrow\\) **won’t you? / will you?** (*Close the door, **won’t you**? / Don’t move, **will you**?*)
  * **Let’s** \\(\rightarrow\\) **shall we?** (*Let’s dance, **shall we**?*)
  * **There is / are** \\(\rightarrow\\) **isn’t / aren’t there?** (*There are many shops, **aren’t there**?*)
  * **This / That is** \\(\rightarrow\\) **isn’t it?** (*That’s your wife over there, **isn’t it**?*)
  * **Everyone / Someone / Anyone / No one** \\(\rightarrow\\) **they** (*Everyone enjoyed the party, **didn’t they**?*)
* **Особливості вимови (Pronunciation Practice):**
  * *aren't you* часто вимовляється як *"aren'tcha"* (/tʃ/ звук).
  * *isn't it* зливається у *"isn'tit"*.
  * *don't you* скорочується до *"don'tya"*.
  * *hasn't she* зліплюється у *"hasnshe"* (/t/ переходить у /ʃ/).

*Example: You were at the webinar, **weren't you**?*
*Example: She doesn't live in London, **does she**?*
*Example: Let's have lunch together, **shall we**?*
*Example: Clean your room, **won't you**?*

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Basic Question Tag Completion

2. You didn’t call her yesterday, **did you**?

### Exercise 2: Matching Halves

1. They are friends, \\(\rightarrow\\) **d) aren’t they?**
2. I am a great student, \\(\rightarrow\\) **b) aren’t I?**
3. Let’s go to the restaurant, \\(\rightarrow\\) **a) shall we?**
4. Help me with this, \\(\rightarrow\\) **c) won’t you?**

### Exercise 3: Choose the Correct Option in Dialogues

**Dialogue 1:**
* A: You're new here, **aren’t you**?
* B: Yes. I started work here yesterday.
* A: Ah. Well, you've met everyone in the office, **haven’t you**?
* B: Yes. There are a lot of people working here, **aren’t there**?

**Dialogue 2:**
* A: You will remember to lock all the doors when you leave, **won’t you**?
* B: Of course. I’m not stupid, **am I**?
* A: No. But you forgot to lock the doors last week, **didn’t you**?
* B: You're not going to let me forget that, **are you**?

**Dialogue 3:**
* A: You haven’t seen Linda lately, **have you**?
* B: I saw her today. I told you, **didn’t I**?
* A: Oh, yes! She didn’t mention the party, **did she**?
* B: No, she didn’t. It’s tomorrow night, **isn’t it**?

### Exercise 4: Correct the Mistakes

1. Sally is good at Maths, isn’t Sally? \\(\rightarrow\\) **Sally is good at Maths, isn’t she?**
2. She never writes to you, doesn’t she? \\(\rightarrow\\) **She never writes to you, does she?** *(Примітка: слово never створює заперечення)*
3. Come back soon, don’t you? \\(\rightarrow\\) **Come back soon, won’t you?**
4. Everybody is here now, isn’t they? \\(\rightarrow\\) **Everybody is here now, aren’t they?**
5. We won’t be late, don’t we? \\(\rightarrow\\) **We won’t be late, will we?**
6. She has a cat, hasn’t she? \\(\rightarrow\\) **She has a cat, doesn’t she?**

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: You like learning English, don't you?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I’d say I really enjoy learning English because it opens up so many international career opportunities.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the topic; I find speaking practice very engaging, but grammar exercises can be somewhat tiring.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I find language learning quite challenging, to be honest, though I understand its professional importance.

**Q: You've been working at your current company for a long time, haven't you?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have been with my company for over four years, and I’ve had a wonderful professional experience here.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I've been here for about two years, which feels like just the right amount of time to get comfortable.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I only joined a few months ago, so I am still adjusting to the team workflow.

**Q: We should take a short break now, shouldn't we?**
* **Positive:** Actually, that sounds like a fantastic idea! Taking a quick break will help us refocus on our tasks.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I don't mind either way; we could take a break now or finish this section first.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid we have a pressing deadline in twenty minutes, so we should probably keep working for a bit longer.

**Q: You won't forget to send the final report today, will you?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I have already prepared the document, so I'll send it over right after our call.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I plan to send it by 5 p.m. once our team lead reviews the financial figures.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I might need until tomorrow morning because I'm still waiting for additional client feedback.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Project Status Verification**
* **Context:** A project lead confirming deliverables with a team member during a weekly sync meeting.
* **Role A:** "Good morning! You've already submitted the client pitch slides, **haven't you**?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, I sent them to the review board yesterday afternoon. You received my email notification, **didn't you**?"
* **Role A:** "Ah, yes, I see it now. The client won't require additional changes before Friday, **will they**?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, they might request minor adjustments, but our team is ready to update the presentation."

**Scenario 2: Coordinating Office Logistics**
* **Context:** Two colleagues organizing seating and AV equipment before a client presentation.
* **Role A:** "We're meeting the external partners in Conference Room B, **aren't we**?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes. There are enough chairs for everyone, **aren't there**?"
* **Role A:** "I believe so. Let's double-check the projector setup before they arrive, **shall we**?"
* **Role B:** "Definitely. Please hold the door for me, **won't you**?"

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to confirm facts, correct minor misunderstandings politely, or give positive updates. |
| **To be honest** | Used when expressing modest hesitation, sharing candid feedback, or admitting uncertainty. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion negative answers, express polite regret, or report minor work delays gently. |
| **Let's..., shall we?** | A polite, collaborative formula for proposing actions, team breaks, or next steps. |
| **..., won't you?** | A soft, courteous way to frame requests or imperatives without sounding demanding. |
| **..., haven't you?** | Used to seek gentle confirmation regarding completed tasks or professional updates. |
