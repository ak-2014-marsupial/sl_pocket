# 23. Adjectives ending in -ed and -ing

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tired** | Feeling a need to rest or sleep as a result of fatigue | Втомлений, стомлений |
| **Tiring** | Causing a feeling of fatigue or exhaustion | Втомлюючий, виснажливий |
| **Interested** | Showing or feeling curiosity or concern about something | Зацікавлений |
| **Interesting** | Holding the attention; causing curiosity or interest | Цікавий |
| **Excited** | Feeling eager, enthusiastic, or full of energy | Схвильований, радісний, у передчутті |
| **Exciting** | Causing great enthusiasm and excitement | Вражаючий, захоплюючий |
| **Satisfied (with)** | Pleased because one has achieved something or got what was wanted | Задоволений (чимось) |
| **Satisfying** | Giving fulfillment or pleasure | Задовільний, що приносить задоволення |
| **Confused** | Unable to think clearly or understand a situation | Спантеличений, збентежений |
| **Confusing** | Hard to understand or unclear | Заплутаний, незрозумілий |
| **Bored** | Feeling weary and restless through lack of interest | Нудьгуючий |
| **Boring** | Not interesting or exciting; tedious | Нудний |
| **Entertained** | Provided with amusement or enjoyment | Розважений |
| **Entertaining** | Amusing or enjoyable | Розважальний |
| **Depressed** | Feeling deeply unhappy or disheartened | Пригнічений, зневірений |
| **Depressing** | Causing feelings of sadness or gloom | Гнітючий, сумний |
| **Amazed** | Extremely surprised or impressed | Вражений, захоплений |
| **Amazing** | Causing great wonder or astonishment | Дивовижний, вражаючий |
| **Exhausted** | Extremely tired, completely drained of physical or mental energy | Виснажений |
| **Exhausting** | Making someone feel completely drained of energy | Виснажливий |
| **Surprised** | Feeling or showing surprise at something unexpected | Здивований |
| **Surprising** | Unexpected or astonishing | Несподіваний, дивовижний |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Use **-ed** when the emotion lives inside your head, but use **-ing** when the cause is out there instead!"

> "People are **bored** by **boring** meetings, but a **satisfying** result leaves everyone **satisfied**."

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula: `Subject + Verb + Adjective (-ed)`
Formula: `Subject / Trigger (Thing / Situation / Person) + Verb + Adjective (-ing)`

* **Прикметники з закінченням -ed:** Описують почуття, емоції та внутрішній стан людини (як людина почувається під впливом зовнішніх обставин).
* **Прикметники з закінченням -ing:** Описують предмети, ситуації, події або людей, які є джерелом (тригером) цих емоцій та викликають відповідні почуття.

*Example: I felt exhausted after the long hike in the mountains.*
*Example: The movie was so boring that I fell asleep after twenty minutes.*
*Example: I am interested in this subject because the lecture is very interesting.*
*Example: The project instructions were confusing, so the whole team felt confused.*

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Fill in the Blanks

3. Emily is **interested** in languages. She likes English, French, and German.

5. The new video game is **exciting**, and everyone wants to play it.

10. After watching the movie, I felt very **entertained** (entertain).
11. The rollercoaster was so **exciting** (excite) that I wanted to ride it again.

2. The movie was so **boring** that I fell asleep.
3. I am **tired** of waiting for the bus.
4. The book was really **depressing** and made me feel sad.

### Translation Block

* Після важкого дня я **втомився** і рано пішов спати. — *After a hard day, I was **tired** and went to sleep early.*
* Довга дорога була **виснажливою**, і Сара заснула. — *The long road was **tiring**, and Sarah fell asleep.*
* Емілі **цікавиться** мовами. Їй подобаються англійська, французька та німецька. — *Emily is **interested** in languages. She likes English, French, and German.*
* Завжди **приємно (приносить задоволення)** успішно завершити складне завдання. — *It’s always **satisfying** to finish a difficult task successfully.*
* Вона **втомилася** чекати на автобус під дощем. — *She was **tired** of waiting for the bus in the rain.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: What is the most amazing place that you visited?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I’d say the most amazing place I’ve ever visited was Kyoto during the spring season. The historic temples and cherry blossoms were completely stunning.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I haven't traveled extensively, but I would consider Prague to be quite impressive due to its historic architecture.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't had the opportunity to visit any truly amazing places yet, though I would certainly love to travel more in the future.

**Q: What is the most interesting movie that you watched? What kind of movies are you interested in?**
* **Positive:** Actually, "Inception" was probably the most interesting movie I’ve seen recently. I'm particularly interested in thought-provoking sci-fi thrillers with complex plots.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it's hard to select just one, but I usually find historical documentaries quite interesting when I have free time.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I don't watch many movies these days, as I find most recent releases rather uninspiring, to be honest.

**Q: What is the most exciting book that you read?**
* **Positive:** Actually, reading "Sapiens" was an remarkably exciting experience for me because it offered a fascinating perspective on human history.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I find classic mystery novels like Sherlock Holmes fairly exciting when I want to relax after work.
* **Negative:** I must admit I haven't read an exciting book in a while, as most technical literature I read for work feels somewhat tiring.

**Q: What do you usually do after a tiring day to relax?**
* **Positive:** Actually, after a long and tiring day at the office, I find it extremely relaxing to go for a peaceful walk in the park or listen to quiet music.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I typically just unwind by preparing a warm cup of tea and watching an entertaining TV show.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I rarely have enough time to relax properly after a tiring day, so I usually just go to bed early to rest.

**Q: When do you usually feel bored?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I rarely feel bored because I always try to keep myself engaged with learning English or working on creative projects.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I only feel a bit bored when I am forced to wait in long queues or sit through lengthy administrative meetings.
* **Negative:** I must admit I tend to feel quite bored whenever I have to perform repetitive data entry tasks without clear objectives.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Project Feedback Meeting**
* **Context:** Discussing a client presentation and project evaluation between two colleagues.
* **Role A:** "Could you tell me how the client presentation went this morning? Were they satisfied with our proposed strategy?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, the initial reaction was quite positive. They found our market analysis very **interesting**, though I'm afraid they felt a bit **confused** by the complex financial timeline."
* **Role A:** "I see. Would it be helpful if we provided a simplified breakdown to make it less **confusing** for them?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, that would be a great idea. It would certainly make the client feel much more **reassured**."

**Scenario 2: Workshop Evaluation**
* **Context:** Evaluating a full-day corporate software training session.
* **Role A:** "Would you say the full-day software integration training was effective for the development team?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, it was somewhat **tiring** because the workshop lasted eight hours, but the practical exercise was extremely **engaging**."
* **Role A:** "That makes sense. Were the junior developers **excited** about implementing the new tools?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes. They found the interactive demos very **inspiring** and feel much more confident now."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to introduce a gentle correction, express a positive surprise, or present an alternative perspective politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when sharing a candid opinion, neutral feedback, or personal preference without sounding overly direct. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion unfavorable news, express polite regret, or deliver mild criticism gently. |
| **I'd say that...** | Used to offer a personal opinion or professional estimate in a modest, non-dogmatic manner. |
| **I must admit...** | Used when acknowledging a personal limitation, minor shortcoming, or unexpected truth politely. |
| **Could / Would / Might** | Modal verbs used to soften requests, recommendations, or hypothetical business scenarios. |
