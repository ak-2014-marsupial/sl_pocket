# 08. Used to (Habits, States & Adaptation)

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Used to + Infinitive** | Expresses past states, regular habits, or repeated actions that no longer occur in the present | Раніше мав звичку / раніше робив |
| **Get used to + V-ing / Noun** | Refers to the gradual process of adapting or becoming accustomed to a new habit, situation, or environment | Призвичаюватися / звикати до чогось |
| **Be used to + V-ing / Noun** | Expresses an established habit or state where something feels familiar and no longer unusual or difficult | Бути звиклим до чогось (мати звичку) |
| **Past situation** | A state of affairs, circumstance, or condition that existed in the past but has changed | Минула ситуація / стан |
| **Adapt to** | To adjust or modify one's behavior and expectations to suit a new environment or condition | Пристосовуватися до |
| **Shift work** | A work pattern where employees work in successive periods or rotating schedules | Робота за змінами |
| **Heavy traffic** | A large volume of vehicles moving along road networks causing slow travel | Интенсивний / важкий дорожній рух |
| **Remote environment** | A professional setup where work is performed outside a central corporate office | Віддалений робочий простір |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Use **used to** when talking about what you did *in the past*, **get used to** for the *process of adapting*, and **be used to** when something feels completely *normal today*!"

> "Remember: **used to** is followed by a plain verb (*infinitive*), but **be/get used to** always requires a noun or a **-ing** form!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Past Habit / State): `Subject + used to + Verb (Infinitive)`
Formula (Negative Past Habit): `Subject + didn't use to + Verb (Infinitive)`
Formula (Question Past Habit): `Did + Subject + use to + Verb (Infinitive)?`
Formula (Process of Adapting): `Subject + get / is getting / got used to + Verb-ing / Noun`
Formula (Established Habit): `Subject + am / is / are / was / were + used to + Verb-ing / Noun`

* **Used to + Infinitive (Минулі звички та стани):**
  Вживається для опису дій, регулярних звичок або тривалих ситуацій у минулому, які більше не відбуваються у теперішньому.
  *Зверніть увагу:* У заперечних реченнях та запитаннях частка *-d* випадає: *didn't use to*, *did you use to...?*

* **Get used to + Verb-ing / Noun (Процес звикання):**
  Описує *процес* пристосування чи адаптації до нових умов. Показыває динаміку: від незвичного до знайомого. Може використовуватися у будь-якому часі (Present Continuous, Past Simple, Future Simple).

* **Be used to + Verb-ing / Noun (Сформована звичка):**
  Описує *результат* звикання. Використовується, коли дія чи обставина вже стала звичною, знайомою та більше не викликає дискомфорту чи стресу.

*Example: I used to live in a small village, but now I live in a big city.*
*Example: She didn't use to drink coffee before university, but now she loves it.*
*Example: I am slowly getting used to waking up at 6 a.m. for my new job.*
*Example: He was used to driving in heavy traffic, so the rush hour didn't stress him out.*

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Remake using "used to" (Affirmative Statements)

1. Emma lived in a small village when she was younger. Now she lives in a big city. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **used to live** in a small village.
2. Jack drank a lot of coffee in university. Now he prefers tea. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **used to drink** a lot of coffee.
3. We went camping every summer when we were children. Now we stay in hotels. \\(\rightarrow\\) We **used to go** camping every summer.

### Exercise 2: Remake using "used to" (Negative Statements)

1. Lisa has a car now, but ten years ago she didn’t have one. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **didn't use to have** a car.
2. Ben loves vegetables now, but he hated them as a child. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **didn't use to like** vegetables.
3. My brother plays video games every day now, but he had no interest in them before. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **didn't use to play** video games.

3. She couldn’t **get used to studying** at her new school at first, but now she feels fine.
4. I am slowly **getting used to getting** up early every day.
5. She is **getting used to working** in a noisy office.
6. We are finally **getting used to eating** healthy food.
7. My parents are **getting used to having** online meetings.

### Exercise 5: Correct the Mistakes

1. She used to eating a lot of junk food, but now she follows a healthy diet. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **used to eat** a lot of junk food, but now she follows a healthy diet.
2. I can’t get used to wake up early for work. \\(\rightarrow\\) I can’t **get used to waking** up early for work.
3. He didn’t used to like vegetables, but now he loves them. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **didn't use to like** vegetables, but now he loves them.
4. We have just moved to a new city, so we are not used to live here yet. \\(\rightarrow\\) We have just moved to a new city, so we are **not used to living** here yet.
5. It is hard for her to used to cooking for herself every day. \\(\rightarrow\\) It is hard for her to **get used to cooking** for herself every day.

### Translation Block

* Вона жила у маленькому селі, коли була молодшою. — *She **used to live** in a small village when she was younger.*
* Мені знадобився час, щоб звикнути до водіння з правої сторони дороги. — *It took me a while to **get used to driving** on the right side of the road.*
* Він не звик жити один, тому що його сусіди з'їжджають лише наступного тижня. — *He **isn't used to living** alone because his roommate is moving out next week.*
* Коли я був дитиною, я дивився мультики щоранку. — *When I was a child, I **used to watch** cartoons every morning.*
* Я звик працювати у галасливому офісі, тому це мене більше не відволікає. — *I **am used to working** in a noisy office, so it doesn't distract me anymore.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: Tell me about something you used to do as a child, but don’t do anymore.**
* **Positive:** Actually, I used to spend entire summer afternoons playing football outside with my neighbors, which was an extraordinarily fun time.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I used to watch a lot of cartoons on weekend mornings, but nowadays I prefer reading news or drinking coffee quietly.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I used to waste quite a bit of time playing video games every evening, though I completely stopped a few years ago.

**Q: Tell me about something you didn't use to like eating when you were younger.**
* **Positive:** Actually, I didn't use to like broccoli or spinach at all as a child, but now I consider them an essential part of my daily diet.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I didn't use to enjoy seafood when I was younger, though I've gradually gotten used to eating fish over time.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I didn't use to eat any cooked vegetables as a kid, and to be fair, I still prefer raw veggie sticks.

**Q: What are some daily professional routines that you are completely used to now?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I am fully used to handling high-volume email streams and managing international online calls every afternoon without feeling overwhelmed.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I am used to logging on around 8:30 a.m. and setting clear priorities before starting complex technical tasks.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I'm still not entirely used to working late during tight sprint deadlines, though I manage when necessary.

**Q: Do you think you could get used to living without the Internet for a month?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I believe I could get used to a digital detox quite easily if I spent that month hiking in the mountains or staying in a peaceful village.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it would take me a few days to get used to living without instant messaging, but I would adapt eventually.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I couldn't get used to living without the Internet at all, as my entire job and communication rely on online tools.

**Q: Do you think you could get used to living and working in another country with a different culture?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I'd say I would enjoy the process of getting used to a new culture and learning local professional customs.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it would probably take me several months to get used to driving on the left or adapting to new social rules.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I would find it quite difficult to get used to being away from my family and familiar home environment.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Adapting to a Remote Work Culture**
* **Context:** A department manager discussing remote work setup and communication with a newly hired team member.
* **Role A:** "Could you tell me how you're finding our remote workflow? Is it difficult to adjust to our online communication tools?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, it was a bit challenging during the first week because I **used to work** in a traditional office environment every day."
* **Role A:** "I completely understand. Have you managed to **get used to organizing** your tasks independently via our project portal?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes. I **am getting used to managing** my calendar digitally now, and I feel much more comfortable with daily async updates."

**Scenario 2: Handling Shift Work & Client Overtime**
* **Context:** Two senior consultants discussing schedule adjustments and managing demanding international clients.
* **Role A:** "Would you be open to taking over the evening client support shifts starting next month?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, I **am not used to working** late hours, so I might find the new schedule somewhat tiring at first."
* **Role A:** "We could adjust your morning duties so you get enough rest. Do you think you would **get used to working** the late shift after a week or two?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, if morning hours are reduced, I'm confident I will **get used to the new schedule** quite quickly."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used when sharing positive personal changes, explaining how you adapted, or clarifying past habits politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when admitting difficulties with adjusting to new schedules, environments, or challenging tasks candidly. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion statements about struggle with adapting to difficult conditions or express hesitation gently. |
| **I'm getting used to...** | A soft, progress-oriented phrase used to communicate ongoing adaptation to new work or life conditions. |
| **It took me a while to...** | Used in professional contexts to describe a successful transition process modestly without sounding incompetent. |
| **I am used to dealing with...** | A confident, professional expression showing experience and familiarity with challenging client situations or tasks. |
