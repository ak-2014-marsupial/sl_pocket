# 24. Spending, Saving and Investing

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cut back on spending** | To spend less money than before by reducing unnecessary expenses | Зменшити / скоротити витрати |
| **Splash out on something** | To spend a large amount of money on something expensive or luxurious | Витратити купу грошей / розтринькатися на щось дороге |
| **Pick up the tab** | To pay the bill for everyone in a group at a restaurant or event | Оплатити рахунок за всіх |
| **Live within your means** | To spend only what you can afford without going into debt | Жити за статками / за своїми коштами |
| **Go over budget** | To spend more money than was originally planned or allocated | Вийти за рамки бюджету / перевищити бюджет |
| **Get a good deal** | To purchase something at a favorable, discounted, or low price | Отримати вигідну пропозицію / вигідно купити |
| **Put money aside** | To reserve or save a portion of income for future use | Відкладати гроші |
| **Cut costs** | To reduce business or personal operational expenses | Скорочувати витрати / кошти |
| **Build up savings** | To gradually accumulate a sum of money over time | Накопичувати заощадження |
| **Put money into an investment** | To allocate funds into financial assets or business ventures to generate future profit | Вкладати гроші в інвестиції |
| **Save for a rainy day** | To save money for unexpected emergencies or future financial difficulties | Відкладати на чорний день |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "If you want to **build up savings** and **save for a rainy day**, you need to **live within your means** and avoid **going over budget**!"

> "It's fine to **splash out on something** special occasionally, provided you usually **cut back on spending** and know how to **get a good deal**!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Financial Phrasal Verbs & Prepositions): `Subject + cut back on / splash out on / put aside + Noun / Verb-ing`  
Formula (Advice & Recommendation): `Subject + should / need to + Verb (Bare Infinitive)`  
Formula (Purpose & Result Clauses): `Subject + Verb + so that / in order to + Verb (Bare Infinitive)`  

* **Фразові дієслова з фіксованими прийменниками (Financial Phrasal Verbs):**  
  Деякі дієслова у сфері фінансів завжди вимагають конкретних прийменників. Після прийменника вживається **іменник** або **герундій (`Verb-ing`)**:
  * **cut back on:** *cut back on spending / cut back on daily coffee*.
  * **splash out on:** *splash out on a luxury holiday / splash out on designer clothes*.
  * **put money into:** *put money into an investment / put money into real estate*.
* **Вираження фінансових застережень та порад (Giving Financial Advice):**  
  Модальні дієслова `should` та `need to` застосовуються для надання порад щодо планування бюджету (*You should live within your means to avoid debt*).
* **Конструкції мети для заощаджень (Clauses of Purpose):**  
  Для пояснення причин накопичення коштів вживаються сполучники `so that` (щоб) або `in order to` (для того щоб) (*Maria puts money aside so that she can buy a house*).

*Example: After reviewing his monthly expenses, Mark decided to cut back on spending.*  
*Example: We wanted our vacation to be memorable, so we decided to splash out on a five-star hotel.*  
*Example: It is essential to save for a rainy day so that unexpected medical bills don't cause stress.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: Do you prefer to splash out on something luxurious occasionally or keep a strict budget at all times?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I believe it's healthy to splash out on something special once in a while, as long as you generally live within your means.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on my current savings goals; if I've reached my targets, I don't mind getting a good deal on a trip.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I avoid splashing out on expensive items altogether, as I prefer to build up savings and feel financially secure.

**Q: How do you usually handle group dinners when the bill arrives — do you split it equally or pick up the tab?**
* **Positive:** Actually, on special celebrations like my birthday, I always prefer to pick up the tab for my friends.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, we usually split the bill equally among everyone to ensure fairness and prevent going over budget.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid picking up the tab for a large group can be overly expensive, so we always pay individually for what we ordered.

**Q: What is your primary strategy to cut costs and save for a rainy day?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I set up automatic transfers every payday to put money aside before I even start spending on daily needs.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, my main approach is to cut back on eating out and look for discounts whenever I need to buy clothes or electronics.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I struggle to cut costs consistently, though I try my best to avoid going over my monthly limit.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Corporate Budget Review & Cost Reduction**
* **Context:** A department head and a financial analyst discussing quarterly operating costs.
* **Role A:** "Could we review our operational performance for Q3? It looks like we exceeded our projected expenditure."
* **Role B:** "To be honest, yes. We **went over budget** by 12% mainly due to unexpected travel and software licensing expenses."
* **Role A:** "What measures can we take in Q4 to correct this imbalance?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, we should **cut costs** by renegotiating supplier contracts and **cut back on spending** for non-essential business travel."

**Scenario 2: Personal Financial Planning & Investment Advice**
* **Context:** A financial advisor discussing long-term wealth accumulation with a client.
* **Role A:** "I've managed to **build up savings** over the past three years, and I'm wondering what my next steps should be."
* **Role B:** "Actually, congratulations! Having an emergency fund to **save for a rainy day** is an essential first step."
* **Role A:** "Should I keep all my extra cash in a standard savings account?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, no. Once you have a safety cushion, it's wise to **put money into an investment** to protect your wealth against inflation."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to share positive financial habits, clarify budget adjustments, or propose savings ideas politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when candidly acknowledging budget overruns, admitting financial doubts, or giving realistic spending feedback. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion news about business cost cuts, budget limits, or declining expensive social invitations gently. |
| **It is wise to...** | A polite, professional formula for recommending financial strategies like investing or emergency savings. |
| **As long as you...** | Used when explaining conditional financial choices, such as indulging in luxury while maintaining overall balance. |
