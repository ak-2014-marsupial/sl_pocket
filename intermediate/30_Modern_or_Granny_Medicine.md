# 30. Modern or 'Granny' Medicine

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Go for regular health check-ups** | To visit a doctor periodically to evaluate overall health and detect problems early | Проходити регулярні медичні обстеження |
| **Get vaccinated against** | To receive an injection that protects the body against a specific disease | Вакцинуватися від |
| **Get a flu shot every year** | To receive an annual influenza vaccine to avoid getting sick in winter | Щороку робити щеплення від грипу |
| **Take painkillers** | To consume medication designed to relieve or reduce pain | Приймати знеболювальні |
| **Take antibiotics** | To take prescribed medicine that destroys or inhibits the growth of bacteria | Приймати антибіотики |
| **Take iron supplements** | To consume dietary iron pills to treat or prevent low iron levels or anemia | Приймати добавки заліза |
| **Heal** | To restore to health, become sound, or repair (used for wounds, cuts, burns, or emotional pain) | Зцілювати, заживати, виліковуватися |
| **Cure** | To completely relieve a person of a disease or medical condition; a substance that cures | Виліковувати, лікувати (хворобу) / ліки |
| **Drink herbal tea** | To consume an infusion of herbs for health or relaxation | Пити трав’яний чай |
| **Drink camomile tea for better sleep** | To drink tea made from dried chamomile flowers to reduce anxiety and promote sleep | Пити ромашковий чай для кращого сну |
| **Breathe in steam to clear a runny nose** | To inhale warm water vapor to unblock nasal passages during a cold | Вдихати пар, щоб прочистити ніс |
| **Rub alcohol on the chest** | To massage rubbing alcohol onto the chest as a traditional remedy for colds | Натирати груди спиртом |
| **Apply aloe vera for burns** | To put aloe vera gel on damaged skin to cool it and reduce pain from burns | Наносити алое вера при опіках |
| **Gargle salt water** | To wash the throat with a mixture of warm water and salt to soothe a sore throat | Полоскати горло солоною водою |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "For medical prevention, **go for regular health check-ups** and **get vaccinated against** infections!"

> "When you have a sore throat, **gargle salt water**, and if you burnt your hand, **apply aloe vera for burns**!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Difference between Heal and Cure):  
`Subject (Wound / Injury / Cut / Body) + heal(s)`  
`Subject (Medicine / Doctor) + cure(s) + Disease / Infection / Cancer`  

Formula (Health Advice with First Conditional):  
`If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + should / can + Verb (Infinitive)`

* **Відмінність між Heal та Cure (Hospital Medicine):**
  * **Heal** описує природний процес загоєння тіла, ран, порізів чи опіків (*Her cut will heal in a few days; time heals emotional pain*).
  * **Cure** означає повне виліковування певної хвороби, інфекції чи діагнозу завдяки лікам чи медичному втручанню (*Scientists are working to cure cancer; antibiotics cured her infection*).
* **Правила вживання медичних дієслів (Medical Collocations):**
  * З медикаментами вживається дієслово **take**: *take painkillers, take antibiotics, take iron supplements*.
  * Зі щепленнями вживається дієслово **get**: *get vaccinated against, get a flu shot*.

*Example: If you have a sore throat, you should gargle salt water three times a day.*  
*Example: You should only take antibiotics if your doctor prescribes them.*  
*Example: Apply aloe vera for burns because it cools the skin and reduces pain.*

### Translation Block

* Вам слід проходити регулярні медичні обстеження, навіть якщо ви почуваєтеся добре. — *You should **go for regular health check-ups**, even if you are feeling fine.*
* Якщо у вас болить горло, полощіть його солоною водою тричі на день. — *If your throat hurts, **gargle salt water** three times a day.*
* Бабуся завжди каже мені пити ромашковий чай для кращого сну. — *My grandma always tells me to **drink camomile tea for better sleep**.*

* Нанесіть алое вера при опіках, щоб охолодити шкіру та зменшити біль. — ***Apply aloe vera for burns** to cool the skin and reduce pain.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: How often do you go for regular health check-ups, and do you get a flu shot every year?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I make it a priority to go for regular health check-ups once a year and always get a flu shot in autumn to stay protected.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I visit the doctor for a general check-up every two years, but I don't always get a flu shot unless required.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I rarely go for regular check-ups unless I feel genuinely unwell, to be honest.

**Q: When you have a cold, do you prefer home remedies like steam inhalation or modern medications?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I prefer home medicine first — I usually breathe in steam to clear a runny nose and drink camomile tea to relax.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the severity; I gargle salt water for a mild sore throat, but I take painkillers if I have a high fever.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid home remedies rarely work quickly enough for me, so I rely on modern pharmaceuticals prescribed by my doctor.

**Q: Have you ever had to take antibiotics and painkillers at the same time?**
* **Positive:** Actually, yes; after my dental surgery last year, my dentist advised me to take antibiotics to cure the infection and painkillers to manage discomfort.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I try to avoid taking antibiotics unless it's a severe bacterial infection, but I occasionally take painkillers for bad headaches.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I haven't needed antibiotics in years, as I prefer resting and letting my body heal naturally whenever possible.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Discussing Sick Leave & Workplace Wellness**
* **Context:** An HR manager and an employee discussing recovery after an acute illness.
* **Role A:** "Good morning! How are you feeling after your sick leave last week?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, I'm feeling much better now. The doctor prescribed a course of treatment, and I had to **take antibiotics** for five days."
* **Role A:** "I'm glad to hear you recovered well. Does our company health plan cover preventative care?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, yes. It's very convenient because employees can **go for regular health check-ups** and **get a flu shot every year** at the clinic."

**Scenario 2: Sharing Health Tips During Travel**
* **Context:** Two colleagues preparing for an international business trip and discussing travel health precautions.
* **Role A:** "Are you ready for our overseas conference next week? Did you check the health requirements?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes. I had to **get vaccinated against** local tropical diseases before obtaining my visa."
* **Role A:** "That's good. Do you carry a first-aid kit when traveling?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, I always pack **painkillers** and herbal teas, and if I get a sore throat, I simply **gargle salt water**."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to introduce positive health choices, share personal recovery stories, or confirm medical details politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when candidly expressing hesitation about medical treatments, admitting rare doctor visits, or sharing neutral habits. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion statements when declining home remedies, expressing concern about an illness, or admitting poor health routines gently. |
| **You should try to...** | A polite, non-intrusive way to suggest home remedies or general health advice to friends or colleagues. |
| **It is important to...** | A professional, respectful formula used to emphasize preventative care, such as check-ups and vaccinations. |
