# 27. Social Media Gossip

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Update status** | To post a new state, photo, or life event on a social media profile | Оновити статус |
| **Follow / Unfollow** | To subscribe to or stop subscribing to someone's social media updates | Підписатися / відписатися |
| **Leave a comment** | To write a response or remark under a social media post | Залишити коментар |
| **Post cryptic messages** | To share mysterious, ambiguous messages online that are hard to understand | Публікувати загадкові повідомлення |
| **Post a picture with someone** | To share a photograph featuring yourself and another person online | Опублікувати фото з кимось |
| **Tag each other / a friend** | To link a person's social media profile to a photo or post | Позначити один одного / друга |
| **Turn off comments** | To disable the comment section on a social media post | Вимкнути коментарі |
| **Change a profile picture** | To replace your primary account photo with a new image | Змінити фото профілю |
| **Delete posts** | To remove previously shared content from a social network profile | Видалити пости |
| **It’s all over the Internet!** | An expression used when a piece of news or content is trending everywhere online | Це всюди в Інтернеті! |
| **Caption** | A short description, title, or explanation written underneath a picture | Підпис (під фото) |
| **Spill the beans** | To reveal secret or private information, often unexpectedly | Розкрити секрет |
| **Bite your tongue** | To stop yourself from saying something that might offend or cause trouble | Тримати язика за зубами |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Before you **spill the beans** online, remember that once a story is out, **it's all over the Internet**!"

> "If you feel like arguing in the comment section, it's usually better to **bite your tongue** or simply **turn off comments**!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Social Media Actions with Infinitives): `Subject + decide(s) / want(s) + to + Verb (unfollow / leave a comment / tag)`  
Formula (Imperative Advice): `Don't forget to + Verb (tag / leave a comment) + Object`  
Formula (Idiomatic Past Actions): `Subject + bit + one's tongue` / `Subject + spilled the beans`  

* **Вживання фразових сполучень у соцмережах (Social Media Collocations):**  
  Дієслова дій у соціальних мережах поєднуються з конкретними прийменниками та іменниками (*leave a comment on a post*, *tag a friend in a picture*, *turn off comments on a post*).
* **Конструкції наміру та рішень (Decisions & Intentions):**  
  Дієслова *decide* та *want* утворюють зв'язок з інфінітивом з часткою `to` (*decided to unfollow*, *decided to turn off comments*, *wanted to spill the beans*).
* **Ідіоматичні вирази стриманості та викриття (Idioms for Gossip & Silence):**  
  * **Spill the beans** означає передчасно або імпульсивно розкрити таємницю.  
  * **Bite one's tongue** описує свідоме утримання від висловлення думки, щоб уникнути конфлікту.

*Example: Don't forget to tag a friend in your post.*  
*Example: She decided to turn off comments because she didn't want negative feedback.*  
*Example: I wanted to tell her the truth, but I bit my tongue.*  

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Complete the Sentences (Fill in the Gaps)

1. I usually **update** status on Facebook when something important happens in my life.
2. Some people **follow** celebrities, then later **unfollow** them if they get bored.
3. If you like my photo, don’t forget to **leave** a comment.
4. She always posts **cryptic** messages on Instagram, and nobody really understands what she means.
5. We decided to **post** a picture with someone after the concert to remember the moment.
6. Friends love to **tag** each other in funny pictures.
7. Some influencers **delete** comments because they don’t want negative feedback.
8. People sometimes **turn off** posts when they regret sharing something online.
9. Did you hear the news? It’s **all over the Internet**!
10. She wrote a funny **caption** under her picture.
11. **Spill** the beans, tell us what really happened!
12. He wanted to say something bad, but decided to **bite** his tongue.

### Exercise 2: Complete the Dialogue

* **Anna:** Hey, did you see Mike’s new profile? He decided to **change a profile picture** yesterday.
* **Tom:** Yeah, I saw it. It looks great! I wanted to **leave a comment**, but the option isn’t there.
* **Anna:** That’s because he decided to **turn off comments**. Too much negativity, he said.
* **Tom:** Oh, I get it. By the way, Sarah and John finally posted a photo together.
* **Anna:** Nice! Did they **tag** each other too?
* **Tom:** Of course! And the funniest thing is their **caption**. It says, "Partners in crime!"
* **Anna:** Oh, I love it. By the way, do you still **follow** that blogger from London?
* **Tom:** No, I decided to **unfollow** him. He shares too many ads.
* **Anna:** Fair enough. Hey, did you hear the news about Kate? **It’s all over the Internet**!
* **Tom:** Really? Tell me! **Spill the beans**, I want the whole story.
* **Anna:** Sorry, I promised her I’d **bite my tongue**.

### Translation Block

* Тобі слід вимкнути коментарі, якщо ти не хочеш бачити негативні відгуки. — *You should **turn off comments** if you don't want to see negative feedback.*
* Вона змінила фото профілю та опублікувала фото з друзями після вечірки. — *She **changed her profile picture** and **posted a picture with** her friends after the party.*
* Не забудь позначити мене на фотографії, яку ми зробили вчора! — *Don't forget to **tag me in the picture** we took yesterday!*
* Він вирішив відписатися від блогера, тому що той публікував занадто багато реклами. — *He decided to **unfollow** the blogger because he shared too many ads.*
* Я хотів розповісти їм все, але вирішив тримати язика за зубами. — *I wanted to tell them everything, but I decided to **bite my tongue**.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: Do you think it's a good idea to turn off comments on social media posts? When and why do people do that?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I'd say turning off comments can be a great way to protect mental health and avoid unnecessary online negativity.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the situation; public figures often turn off comments during controversial events, while regular users rarely need to.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I don't see the point of turning off comments, as social media is meant for open communication and feedback.

**Q: Why do people change their profile pictures or post cryptic messages so often?**
* **Positive:** Actually, changing a profile picture is just a fun way to share a new life chapter or a memorable moment with friends.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, people post cryptic messages when they want attention or empathy without revealing full private details.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I find cryptic messages somewhat annoying, as nobody really understands what the person means.

**Q: Has anyone ever spilled the beans about a surprise or secret that was meant for you?**
* **Positive:** Actually, a friend accidentally spilled the beans about my surprise birthday party last year, but we both laughed about it.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, people sometimes struggle to bite their tongue when they are excited about big news.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I dislike it when people can't keep a secret, so I try to be very careful with who I trust.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Managing Brand Social Media & Community Comments**
* **Context:** A SMM manager and a PR specialist discussing comment moderation on a company's product announcement.
* **Role A:** "Have you reviewed the user responses under our new product launch photo?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes! Customers loved the announcement, and many decided to **tag their friends** in the post."
* **Role A:** "That's fantastic engagement! Did we receive any inappropriate spam?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, a few bot accounts left spam. We deleted those messages, but we certainly won't **turn off comments** completely."

**Scenario 2: Handling Confidential Corporate News**
* **Context:** Two corporate communications specialists reviewing media leaks before an official press release.
* **Role A:** "Did you see the latest industry blog post about our upcoming merger?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, yes; **it's all over the Internet** already!"
* **Role A:** "Do we know who revealed the details to journalists?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, someone in management accidentally **spilled the beans** during an informal interview, instead of **biting their tongue** until Friday."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to share positive social media experiences, clarify online facts, or offer polite suggestions. |
| **To be honest** | Used when candidly expressing opinions on online behavior, cryptic posts, or privacy choices. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion negative views about social media drama, spam, or unhelpful online trends gently. |
| **Fair enough** | A polite, conversational formula for validating someone else's decision (e.g., unfollowing an account). |
| **It is a good idea to...** | Used when recommending healthy online habits, such as limiting screen time or moderating comments. |
