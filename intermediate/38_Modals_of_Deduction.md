# 38. Modals of Deduction

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Modals of deduction** | Modal verbs used to express how certain or sure you are about a present or past situation based on evidence | Модальні дієслова припущення / дедукції |
| **High certainty** | A high degree of confidence (90–99%) that something is true based on strong evidence | Висока впевненість / майже 100% впевненість |
| **Probability / Possibility** | A moderate level of likelihood (50–70%) that something is true or possible | Ймовірність / можливість |
| **Impossibility** | A complete conviction (0%) that something is logically impossible or untrue | Неможливість |
| **Deduce / Make a deduction** | To reach a logical conclusion based on available facts or visual evidence | Робити дедуктивний висновок / припущення |
| **Health-conscious** | Mindful of one's health and choosing healthy food or lifestyle habits | Той, хто піклується про здоров'я |
| **Leave in a hurry** | To depart from a place very quickly due to emergency or lack of time | Поспіхом виходити / залишати дім |
| **Work late** | To continue working past regular office hours or into the night | Працювати допізна |
| **Look pale** | Having a lighter skin tone than usual due to illness, exhaustion, or shock | Виглядати блідим |
| **Fire drill / Alarm** | An emergency evacuation practice or warning signal in a building | Навчальна тривога / евакуація |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Use **must** when you are 99% sure something is true, **might/may/could** when it's possible, and **can't** when it's logically impossible!"

> "When making deductions about continuous actions happening *right now*, use the continuous infinitive: **modal + be + V-ing** (*She must be working*)!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (High Certainty — 90–99%): `Subject + must + Verb (Bare Infinitive / be + V-ing)`  
Formula (Probability — 50–70%): `Subject + may / might / could + Verb (Bare Infinitive / be + V-ing)`  
Formula (Impossibility — 0%): `Subject + can't + Verb (Bare Infinitive / be + V-ing)`  

* **Must (Висока впевненість — 90–99%):** Вживається, коли ми майже на 100% впевнені у своєму висновку на основі наявних фактів чи доказів («це точно так», «по-іншому бути не може»).
* **May / Might / Could (Ймовірність — 50–70%):** Вживаються, коли дія є можливою, але ми не впевнені до кінця («можливо», «може бути»).
* **Can't (Неможливість — 0%):** Вживається для вираження категоричної заперечної впевненості, коли щось є логічно неможливим («не може бути, щоб...»). *Зверніть увагу: запереченням до must у значенні дедукції є саме can't, а не mustn't!*
* **Simple vs. Continuous Infinitive після модальних дієслів:**
  * **Simple Infinitive (`modal + V`):** Для опису постійних станів, фактів або регулярних дій (*He must know the truth*).
  * **Continuous Infinitive (`modal + be + V-ing`):** Для опису процесів, які відбуваються безпосередньо в момент мовлення (*She must be sleeping right now*).

*Example: It's 10 a.m. and he doesn't pick up the phone. He must be at work.*  
*Example: She has circles under her eyes. She might be tired.*  
*Example: He works on Sunday. He can't be happy about it!*  
*Example: Look at her! She has headphones on, so she must be listening to music.*  

## 4. Written Practice

### Exercise 1: Choose the Correct Option (Must / Might / Can't)

1. He always wears a suit and leaves home early. He **must** work in an office. I am sure.
2. They never open the curtains. They **can't** like sunlight. It’s impossible.
3. Their children are so polite. They **must** have very good parents. That’s for sure.
4. They **might** live near the park. I often see them walking their dog there. But I am not sure.
5. I’m not sure where Tom is. He **might** be at the gym, or maybe he’s still at home.
6. The restaurant is empty on a Saturday night. It **can't** be very popular.
7. They just moved here last week, so they **can't** know many people yet.
8. They **can't** be at school now — it’s Sunday!
9. That **can't** be true! I read a completely different story online.

### Exercise 2: Fill in the Gaps with "must / might / can't"

1. The lights are off, and it’s very quiet. They **must** be asleep for sure.
2. He’s smiling and talking to everyone. I think he **might** be a friendly person.
3. That boy looks too young. He **can't** be a teacher. He is only 15 years old.
4. He’s looking around confusedly. He **might** be lost, but I don’t know.
5. I don’t see his car outside. He **can't** be at home. It is impossible.
6. She never drinks coffee. She **can't** like it.
7. He is eating a lot of vegetables. I am sure he **must** be very health-conscious.
8. That girl looks so much like Emma. She **might** be her sister, or not.

### Exercise 3: Paraphrase using "must be / can't be / might be" (Continuous & Present)

1. I’m sure he is cooking dinner now. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **must be cooking** dinner.
2. I think she is reading a book, but I’m not certain. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **might be reading** a book now.
3. I don’t see her in the office. She is definitely at home. \\(\rightarrow\\) She **must be working** from home today.
4. He is on a flight to Paris. \\(\rightarrow\\) He **can't be driving** a car right now.
5. She has headphones on and is nodding her head. I am sure she **must be listening** to music.
6. They are running in sports clothes. I think they **might be playing** football.
7. He is asleep in his bed. He **can't be studying** for the exam.
8. She’s lying on the sofa with her eyes closed. She **must be resting**.

### Exercise 4: Simple or Continuous Infinitive after Modals

1. She never eats chocolate. She **can't like** sweets.
2. He is always very polite. He **must have** good manners.
3. I don’t see her at home. She **can't be sleeping**.
4. Listen! She **must be talking** to someone on the phone.
5. He never drinks coffee. He **might like** tea more.
6. I can’t see his car outside. He **must be** at work now.
7. She **can't be studying** for her exams — She is sick.
8. They **might know** a lot of people — they’ve been very friendly to everyone.
9. She is very quiet. She **might be doing** something important.
10. He **must work** very hard — that’s why he is always tired.

### Translation Block

* Вона точно працює допізна — світло в її кабінеті досі горить. — *She **must be working** late again — her light is still on.*
* Можливо, він бреше — його історія звучить дивно. — *He **might be lying** — his story sounds strange.*
* Не може бути, щоб вони говорили серйозно — ця ідея просто безглузда! — *They **can't be serious** — that idea is ridiculous!*
* Ти точно знаєш відповідь, адже це дуже просто. — *You **must know** the answer — it's really easy.*
* Можливо, він погано почувається — він виглядає блідим. — *He **might not be feeling** well — he looks pale.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: What do you think when you see your colleague's office light still on late at night?**
* **Positive:** Actually, she must be finalizing the urgent quarterly report because our client deadline is tomorrow morning.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, she might be working late, or she could have simply forgotten to turn off the light before logging off.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid she can't be resting properly if she stays at the office until 10 p.m. every evening.

**Q: What would you deduce if you saw everybody standing outside an office building during work hours?**
* **Positive:** Actually, there must be a scheduled fire drill taking place, as everyone seems calm and organized.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, they might be waiting for a VIP guest to arrive, or there could be a minor technical issue inside the building.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid something unexpected must have happened with the building's power grid or safety systems.

**Q: What would you think if a friend suddenly didn't answer calls and left 20 "call me" messages?**
* **Positive:** Actually, something urgent must have come up, or she might have received some extraordinary news she wants to share right away.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, she might be experiencing a minor emergency, so I should call her back as soon as possible.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid she must be facing a difficult situation if she tried reaching me so many times in a row.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Assessing a Delayed Project Release**
* **Context:** A project manager and a tech lead analyzing why a software team hasn't updated their portal.
* **Role A:** "Good morning! The engineering team hasn't uploaded the updated build yet. Do you know what happened?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, they **must be testing** the final security patch right now because the server logs show high activity."
* **Role A:** "Could they be having trouble with the main database?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, they **can't be having** database issues because all primary systems are running smoothly. They **might be wrapping up** code reviews."

**Scenario 2: Investigating Unexpected Office Circumstances**
* **Context:** Two colleagues noticing an empty department desk during core business hours.
* **Role A:** "Have you seen Alex today? His computer is on, but his desk has been empty all morning."
* **Role B:** "To be honest, he **must be attending** the client workshop on the third floor since his meeting calendar was blocked."
* **Role A:** "Could he have gone home sick?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, he **can't be at home** because his jacket and car keys are still sitting right here on his chair."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to state logical deductions politely, share strong observations, or gently correct assumptions. |
| **To be honest** | Used when expressing moderate probability, sharing candid thoughts, or acknowledging uncertainty. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion negative deductions, express concern about a problem, or report negative evidence softly. |
| **He / She must be...** | A confident, evidence-based deduction formula used to express near certainty (90–99%). |
| **They might / could be...** | A polite, tentative formula for expressing logical possibility (50–70%) without overcommitting. |
| **It can't be...** | A logical formula used to state that a scenario or explanation is impossible (0%). |
