# 15. Don't Fall for All Ads

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Too good to be true** | So attractive or favorable that it seems unbelievable or dishonest | Занадто добре, щоб бути правдою |
| **Limited-time offer** | A special deal or price available only for a short period of time | Пропозиція на обмежений час |
| **Overpriced** | Costing much more than the actual value or reasonable price | Завищений за ціною |
| **A knockoff** | An unauthorized, cheap copy or imitation of a branded product | Підробка |
| **A good deal** | A product or service purchased at a very favorable price | Вигідна угода |
| **Advertisement / advert / ad** | A public notice or promotion promoting a product, service, or event | Реклама |
| **Fall for a scam** | To be deceived or tricked into losing money or personal information | Повестися на шахрайство |
| **Get tricked** | To be intentionally deceived or misled by someone | Бути обдуреним |
| **Pay upfront** | To pay money before receiving the agreed goods or services | Платити наперед |
| **Sound fishy** | To seem suspicious, questionable, or unlikely to be honest | Звучати підозріло |
| **Free trial** | A temporary test period offered to customers before purchasing a subscription | Безкоштовний пробний період |
| **Scam calls / texts** | Fraudulent phone calls or messages aimed at stealing money or data | Шахрайські дзвінки / повідомлення |
| **Deepfake** | AI-generated synthetic video or audio mimicking a real person | Діпфейк |
| **Don't believe the hype** | A warning to remain skeptical about excessive public praise or excitement | Не вірте хайпу (ажіотажу) |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "If an online offer looks **too good to be true**, don't **fall for a scam**—it's likely a **knockoff** or a fake deal!"

> "Never **pay upfront** when an email **sounds fishy**, and don't forget to cancel a **free trial** before you get charged!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (Passive Caution with Get): `Subject + get / got + Verb3 (tricked / scammed)`  
Formula (Perception & Evaluation): `Subject + sound(s) / look(s) + Adjective / Noun Phrase (fishy / overpriced / too good to be true)`  
Formula (Prohibition & Warning): `Subject + shouldn't + Verb (pay upfront / click links)` / `Don't + Verb (believe the hype)`  

* **Пасивний стан з дієсловом Get (Passive with Get):**  
  Конструкція `get + V3` (*get tricked, get scammed*) виражає дію, коли людина стає жертвою обману або шахрайства. Вона є дуже поширеною в неформальному та розмовному мовленні.
* **Оцінка вірогідності та сумніву (Expressing Suspicion & Doubt):**  
  Дієслова сприйняття `sound` та `look` у поєднанні з прикметниками (*sound fishy, look too good to be true*) дозволяють висловити підозру щодо рекламних обіцянок чи сумнівних повідомлень.
* **Застереження та рекомендації (Cautionary Advice):**  
  Модальне дієслово `shouldn't` уживається для надання порад щодо фінансової безпеки в інтернеті (*you shouldn't pay upfront*).

*Example: Tourists often get tricked by fake taxi drivers.*  
*Example: That online sale looks too good to be true — maybe it's a scam.*  
*Example: You shouldn't pay upfront for a service from someone you don't know.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: Have you ever fallen for a scam because something looked too good to be true in an advertisement?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I once got tricked by a Black Friday online sale where a designer coat looked like a good deal, but it turned out to be a cheap knockoff.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I almost fell for a scam when I received a scam text about a parcel delivery, but the link sounded fishy so I deleted it.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I've never fallen for a scam, as I always verify online seller reviews before making any payment.

**Q: Would you ever pay upfront for a product that seems like a good deal, or would it sound fishy to you?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I don't mind paying upfront if I'm purchasing from a verified store with clear buyer protection policies.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the platform; paying upfront for major retail deals is fine, but paying an unknown individual always sounds fishy.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I would never pay upfront to an unverified online seller, as it feels far too risky.

**Q: Have you or someone you know ever got tricked by scam calls or scam texts?**
* **Positive:** Actually, my relative once got tricked by a scam call claiming to be from a bank, but fortunately she blocked her card immediately.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, I receive scam texts fairly often, but I simply report the numbers and never click on unverified links.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid nobody in my immediate circle has been tricked by scam calls, as we are quite cautious regarding digital security.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Reviewing Vendor Quotes & Pricing Suspicion**
* **Context:** A procurement officer and a marketing manager reviewing a unusually cheap supplier proposal.
* **Role A:** "Could we discuss the software vendor proposal we received this morning? The pricing seems surprisingly low."
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes! The quote looks **too good to be true**, and requiring us to **pay upfront** sounds quite **fishy**."
* **Role A:** "Agreed. Should we request a background security check before proceeding?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, that's essential. We shouldn't **fall for a scam** or end up buying an **overpriced** software **knockoff**."

**Scenario 2: Addressing Cybersecurity & Phishing Prevention**
* **Context:** An IT security lead briefing corporate employees on recent phishing attempts.
* **Role A:** "Have any team members reported receiving suspicious emails or messages this week?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, several employees received **scam texts** offering a **free trial** for AI design tools."
* **Role A:** "Did anyone click the link or enter company login credentials?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, no one **got tricked**. Everyone realized the offer sounded **fishy** and reported the messages promptly."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to share personal experiences with online deals, gently correct assumptions, or report security findings politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when admitting past shopping mistakes, expressing doubt about suspicious offers, or sharing candid feedback gracefully. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion statements when refusing upfront payments, declining risky offers, or reporting fraud attempts gently. |
| **It sounds a bit fishy to me** | A polite, non-confrontational formula for expressing suspicion about an offer without sounding overly aggressive. |
| **I'd suggest verifying... before...** | A professional, courteous way to advise colleagues or clients against rushing into unverified financial transactions. |
