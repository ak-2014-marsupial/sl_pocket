# 25. Gossip and Rumors

## 1. Vocabulary & Definitions

| Word/Phrase | Definition in English | Переклад |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Date someone** | To be in a romantic or dating relationship with someone | Зустрічатися з кимось |
| **Break up with someone** | To end a romantic relationship with a person | Розійтися / розірвати стосунки з кимось |
| **Have a secret crush** | To secretly like or be attracted to someone without telling them | Мати таємне захоплення (краша) |
| **Cheat on someone** | To be romantically or sexually unfaithful to one's partner | Зраджувати когось |
| **Get promoted** | To be raised to a higher position, rank, or role at work | Отримати підвищення |
| **Get fired** | To be dismissed or terminated from a job due to mistakes or cuts | Бути звільненим (з роботи) |
| **Get a raise** | To receive an increase in one's salary or hourly pay | Отримати підвищення зарплати |
| **Quit suddenly** | To leave one's employment unexpectedly without long notice | Раптово звільнитися |
| **Have an office romance** | To have a romantic relationship with a coworker at work | Мати службовий роман |
| **Talk behind someone's back** | To say negative or critical things about a person without their knowledge | Говорити за чиєюсь спиною |
| **A two-faced person** | Someone who acts friendly to your face but says bad things behind your back | Лицемірна людина |
| **Vanish from social media** | To suddenly delete accounts or stop using all social media platforms | Зникнути з соціальних мереж |
| **To whisper** | To speak extremely quietly using breath rather than vocal cords | Шепотіти / шепіт |
| **To gossip** | To talk about other people's private lives, often spreading unverified rumors | Пліткувати / плітки |

## 2. Vocabulary Hooks

> "Be careful with who you trust at work—a **two-faced person** might **gossip** and **talk behind your back**!"

> "Don't **quit suddenly** over an **office romance**; work hard and focus on **getting a raise** instead!"

## 3. Grammar Essentials

Formula (First Conditional for Workplace & Relationship Outcomes): `If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + will / can / might + Verb (Bare Infinitive)`  
Formula (Cause & Effect in Gossip): `Subject + Verb (Present/Past) + because / after + Clause`  

* **Перший тип умовних речень у робочих та особистих ситуаціях (First Conditional):**  
  Конструкція `If + Present Simple, Subject + will / might / can + Verb` вживається для опису реальних майбутніх наслідків чи умовних ситуацій на роботі та в стосунках (*If I work harder, I might get promoted next month*).
* **Причинно-наслідкові зв'язки у розповідях (Cause & Effect):**  
  Сполучники *because*, *after*, та *when* допомагають пояснювати причини пліток, звільнень чи розриву стосунків (*They broke up after five years of being together*).
* **Фразові дієслова із заперечними та позитивними значеннями:**  
  * **break up with** — розійтися з кимось.  
  * **cheat on** — зраджувати когось (*значення вимагає прийменника on*).  
  * **talk behind one's back** — говорити за спиною (*значення вимагає прийменника behind*).  

*Example: If I work harder, I might get promoted next month.*  
*Example: He asked his boss to get a raise after finishing a big project.*  
*Example: If two coworkers start dating, they have an office romance.*  
*Example: She broke up with her boyfriend because he cheated on her.*

### Translation Block

* Вона дізналася, що її хлопець зраджував їй. — *She found out that her boyfriend **cheated on** her.*
* Він попросив керівника про підвищення зарплати після того, як завершив великий проєкт. — *He asked his boss to **get a raise** after finishing a big project.*
* Колеги пліткували та розмовляли за її спиною. — *Colleagues were **gossiping** and talking **behind her back**.*
* Вона раптово звільнилася з роботи і навіть не попрощалася з колегами. — *She **quit suddenly** and didn't even say goodbye to her coworkers.*

## 5. Speaking & Discussion Guide

**Q: How do you usually handle workplace gossip or coworkers talking behind someone's back?**
* **Positive:** Actually, I always try to steer the conversation away from personal rumors and focus on professional tasks.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on the situation; if it's minor chatter, I simply ignore it, but I avoid repeating unverified rumors.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid I find office gossip quite toxic, to be honest, so I step away whenever colleagues start whispering.

**Q: What would you do if you found out a coworker was having a secret office romance?**
* **Positive:** Actually, as long as it doesn't affect their work performance or team dynamics, I respect their personal privacy completely.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, office romances can complicate team relationships, so keeping work and romance separate is usually wise.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid office romances often lead to awkward drama if the couple breaks up, so I prefer maintaining clear professional boundaries.

**Q: Do you think it's better to ask directly to get a raise or wait for your manager to offer it?**
* **Positive:** Actually, if you have achieved strong project results, it's very reasonable to schedule a meeting and ask to get a raise directly.
* **Neutral:** To be honest, it depends on company policy; I prefer bringing up salary reviews during scheduled annual performance appraisals.
* **Negative:** I'm afraid asking directly for a raise can feel intimidating, to be honest, so many employees wait until they get promoted.

## 6. Professional Scenarios

**Scenario 1: Addressing Office Gossip & Professional Ethics**
* **Context:** Two senior colleagues discussing how to handle rumors within their department.
* **Role A:** "Have you noticed some team members whispering in the conference room this morning?"
* **Role B:** "To be honest, yes. They were spreading rumors about who might **get fired** after the company restructuring."
* **Role A:** "We shouldn't let people **talk behind someone's back**; it damages team morale."
* **Role B:** "Actually, I agree completely. It's much better to focus on work so everyone has a chance to **get promoted**."

**Scenario 2: Discussing Career Growth & Salary Negotiations**
* **Context:** A team member discussing career trajectory with a mentor.
* **Role A:** "I've completed several major client projects over the past year. Should I schedule a meeting with management?"
* **Role B:** "Actually, yes! If you've consistently delivered great results, you should ask to **get a raise**."
* **Role A:** "I'm worried my manager might think I want to **quit suddenly** if I bring up salary."
* **Role B:** "To be honest, as long as you present your achievements professionally, they will appreciate your commitment."

## 7. Polite English Cheat Sheet

| Phrase / Softener | When to use it (Context) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Actually** | Used to correct misunderstandings gently, offer positive career advice, or confirm facts politely. |
| **To be honest** | Used when candidly expressing concerns about office gossip, sharing sensitive views, or giving modest feedback. |
| **I'm afraid (that)** | Used to cushion statements when declining gossip, expressing concern about office drama, or discussing difficult workplace news. |
| **As long as it doesn't affect...** | A polite, fair formula for commenting on coworkers' personal choices without being judgmental. |
| **It is much better to...** | A constructive, professional phrase used when suggesting ethical behavior or focusing on career growth. |
